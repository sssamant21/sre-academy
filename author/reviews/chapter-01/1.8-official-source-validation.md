## Validation Summary

No blocking factual issue was found in the Chapter 1.8 author draft.

The draft is aligned with official Kubernetes documentation on these points:

- Kubernetes production environments require availability, scale, security, access management, and resource planning.
- Kubernetes workload controllers manage sets of Pods and replace Pods rather than repairing individual Pods forever.
- Pods have lifecycle states and conditions that affect availability and routing.
- Startup, readiness, and liveness probes serve different reliability purposes.
- Readiness failure removes Pods from Service endpoints through endpoint readiness behavior.
- Liveness probes can be useful but must be configured carefully to avoid harmful restarts.
- Disruptions can be voluntary or involuntary.
- PodDisruptionBudgets help manage voluntary disruptions but do not prevent every failure mode.
- Resource requests affect scheduling, and limits affect runtime enforcement.
- Node pressure can lead to evictions.
- Topology and placement controls influence failure-domain resilience.
- Deployments support rolling updates and rollout status behavior.
- Autoscaling depends on metrics and available capacity.
- Control-plane high availability requires deliberate topology and operational planning in self-managed clusters.
