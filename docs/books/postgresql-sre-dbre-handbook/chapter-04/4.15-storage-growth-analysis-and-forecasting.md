# 4.15 -- Storage Growth Analysis and Forecasting

**Status:** Complete -- Revised Final / Canonical Edition

## Purpose

Capacity engineering requires time-series growth analysis, not isolated size snapshots. Forecasting must combine PostgreSQL object growth with actual infrastructure occupancy and known business or maintenance events.

## Growth Model

```text
Current allocation
 + organic data growth
 + MVCC/churn envelope
 + index/TOAST growth
 + WAL/temp/maintenance envelope
 + safety reserve
 = future capacity requirement
```

## Baselines

Collect database, schema, heap, index, TOAST, tablespace, WAL, temporary storage, filesystem/PVC/provider capacity, IOPS, throughput, and latency on a consistent cadence. Preserve enough history to identify daily, weekly, monthly, seasonal, release-driven, and batch-driven patterns.

## Forecasting

A basic runway calculation is:

```text
runway = usable free capacity / positive net occupancy growth rate
```

Use infrastructure occupancy for infrastructure runway. Do not substitute logical row growth or dead-tuple estimates.

Use multiple horizons: short-term incident runway, medium-term procurement/expansion runway, and long-term architecture runway. Model known step changes such as migrations, backfills, retention changes, new customers, index creation, major upgrades, or historical loads separately from organic growth.

## Growth Queries

PostgreSQL exposes current size, while the monitoring platform should persist history:

```sql
SELECT datname, pg_database_size(datname) AS bytes
FROM pg_database
ORDER BY bytes DESC;
```

```sql
SELECT schemaname, relname,
       pg_total_relation_size(relid) AS total_bytes
FROM pg_stat_user_tables
ORDER BY total_bytes DESC;
```

## Forecast Quality

Do not extrapolate from one short abnormal window. Separate sustained trend from temporary spikes, rewrites, bulk loads, temporary files, WAL retention incidents, and backup artifacts. Maintain forecast confidence bands or conservative scenarios where uncertainty is material.

## Headroom and Intervention

Forecast the date at which operational headroom will be breached, not merely the date of theoretical 100% utilization. Include expansion lead time, validation time, maintenance/rewrite working space, failover requirements, and provider/storage scaling constraints.

## Failure Scenarios

**Linear forecast misses step load:** a planned migration adds hundreds of GB instantly. Correct by maintaining event-adjusted forecasts.

**Database-size forecast misses WAL/temp:** object growth appears safe while `pg_wal` or temporary workload exhausts the volume. Forecast independent storage domains.

**Percentage-only planning:** 80% of a 100 GB volume and 80% of a 20 TB volume have radically different absolute headroom and response options. Track both percentage and free bytes/runway.

## Production Rules

1. Persist historical size and infrastructure occupancy.
2. Forecast actual storage consumption, not only logical data.
3. Separate organic growth from step events and transient spikes.
4. Track multiple storage domains independently.
5. Forecast headroom breach before disk exhaustion.
6. Include expansion lead time and maintenance working space.
7. Reforecast after migrations, purges, schema/index changes, and incidents.
8. Compare forecast with actuals and improve the model continuously.
