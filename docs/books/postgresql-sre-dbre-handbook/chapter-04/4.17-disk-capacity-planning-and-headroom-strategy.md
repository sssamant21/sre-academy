# 4.17 -- Disk Capacity Planning and Headroom Strategy

**Status:** Complete -- Revised Final / Canonical Edition

## Purpose

PostgreSQL must operate with intentional storage headroom. The usable ceiling is lower than theoretical device capacity because production requires room for normal growth, WAL and temporary bursts, maintenance, rewrites, backups where applicable, failover behavior, and emergency recovery.

## Capacity Envelope

```text
Live persistent data
+ indexes / TOAST
+ reusable operating envelope
+ WAL envelope
+ temp envelope
+ maintenance/rewrite headroom
+ forecast growth
+ emergency reserve
= required capacity
```

## Headroom Strategy

Do not standardize one percentage for every platform. Define warning, critical, and intervention levels from absolute free capacity, growth rate, runway, expansion lead time, workload burstiness, storage scaling behavior, and largest credible maintenance operation.

A useful principle is to trigger intervention while enough capacity remains to execute the safest recovery option.

## Runway

```text
runway = usable free bytes / positive net growth bytes per unit time
```

Calculate both normal-growth runway and stressed runway. Include known upcoming loads and maintenance events.

## Independent Failure Domains

Where architecture separates tablespaces, WAL, temporary storage, or backup staging, calculate headroom independently for each underlying volume. Aggregate percentages can hide a single volume approaching exhaustion.

## Cloud and Kubernetes

For managed databases and cloud disks, account for provider autoscaling rules, maximum storage, modification latency, IOPS/throughput coupling, and service-specific restrictions. For Kubernetes, validate PVC expansion, StorageClass support, filesystem growth, operator behavior, topology, and quota/ephemeral-storage limits.

## Failure Scenarios

**Expansion requested too late:** capacity crosses critical threshold before provider or change process completes. Alert on runway plus lead time.

**Enough free space for steady state, not maintenance:** a rewrite or index build exhausts remaining capacity. Reserve maintenance headroom explicitly.

**Aggregate capacity masks WAL volume:** data volume is healthy while a separate WAL filesystem fills. Monitor every storage domain independently.

## Production Runbook

1. Measure free bytes and percentage.
2. Calculate recent and stressed growth rates.
3. Determine runway to intervention and exhaustion.
4. Identify dominant growth sources.
5. Check upcoming migrations, purges, backups, index builds, and upgrades.
6. Validate expansion capability and lead time.
7. Expand before emergency thresholds where justified.
8. Confirm IOPS/throughput after capacity changes.
9. Update forecasts and documented limits.

## Production Rules

1. 100% utilization is never the operational target.
2. Headroom must support recovery and maintenance, not only organic growth.
3. Use percentage, absolute free bytes, and runway together.
4. Include expansion lead time in alert design.
5. Protect independent storage domains separately.
6. Validate cloud/PVC maximums and scaling behavior before an incident.
7. Recalculate headroom after major workload or architecture changes.
