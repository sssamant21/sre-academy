# 5.24 -- Transaction/Concurrency Troubleshooting and Production Runbooks

**Status:** Complete -- Revised Final / Canonical Edition

## Blocking Incident Runbook
1. Confirm application symptom and incident start.
2. Capture `pg_stat_activity`, `pg_locks`, blocking PIDs, wait events, and transaction age.
3. Build the blocking chain and identify root blocker.
4. Identify application/owner and business operation.
5. Determine lock mode/object and rollback cost.
6. Prefer application-safe cancellation when sufficient; terminate only when required and approved.
7. Monitor rollback, queue drain, replicas, CPU/I/O, and SLO recovery.
8. Preserve SQL/log evidence and complete RCA.

## Deadlock Runbook
Collect deadlock logs and involved statements; reconstruct lock ordering; confirm retry behavior; fix inconsistent ordering or transaction scope; regression-test concurrently.

## Old-Transaction Runbook
Identify backend XID/xmin, state, owner, locks, vacuum/storage impact, and prepared transactions. Remove blockers safely and verify vacuum/horizon recovery.

## Automation
Automate detection, graph construction, evidence collection, ticket enrichment, and dashboards. Human/change approval should normally govern production backend termination and transaction-affecting remediation.

## Rules
1. Evidence before intervention when SLO permits.
2. Root cause before random session kills.
3. Verify recovery after remediation.
4. Every severe concurrency incident produces prevention work.
5. Runbooks must include application ownership and escalation.
