# 6.11 -- Nested Loop, Hash, and Merge Join Strategies

**Status:** Complete -- Revised Final / Canonical Edition

Nested-loop joins can be excellent when the outer side is small and the inner side has efficient parameterized access. Hash joins are useful for equality joins when building/probing a hash table is economical. Merge joins can be effective when inputs are sorted or naturally ordered and the join condition supports merging.

A bad join choice is frequently a symptom of wrong cardinality estimates rather than a defective join algorithm. Diagnose estimated-vs-actual rows, join order, indexes, statistics, memory/spill, and data skew. Do not permanently disable join methods as a generic fix; use such settings only as controlled diagnostic experiments.
