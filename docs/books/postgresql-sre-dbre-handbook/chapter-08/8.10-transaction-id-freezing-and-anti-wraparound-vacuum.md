# 8.10 — Transaction ID Freezing and Anti-Wraparound Vacuum

Status: **CANONICAL**

## Purpose

Transaction-ID freezing is a PostgreSQL correctness and availability mechanism, not merely VACUUM housekeeping. PostgreSQL uses finite 32-bit normal transaction IDs (XIDs) for MVCC visibility. As XIDs advance, old live tuple transaction information must eventually be frozen so that wraparound cannot make its age ambiguous.

The production objective is not simply to keep autovacuum running. It is to ensure that freezing horizons advance, maintenance completes before correctness deadlines, and sufficient XID runway remains.

## 8.10.1 XID Architecture and Wraparound

The normal `xid` space contains approximately 2^32, or 4.29 billion, values. PostgreSQL uses wraparound-aware comparisons, giving a conceptual horizon of roughly two billion transactions on either side of a normal XID. This theoretical horizon is not an operational target; PostgreSQL deliberately forces maintenance much earlier.

## 8.10.2 What Freezing Means

When an old tuple becomes eligible for freezing, PostgreSQL records that its creating transaction is sufficiently old that ordinary future XID wraparound comparison no longer determines the tuple's committed age. Modern PostgreSQL normally preserves the tuple's original `xmin` while recording frozen status.

Freezing and dead-tuple cleanup solve different problems:

```text
dead-tuple cleanup != transaction-ID freezing
```

A tuple can be live, very old, and eligible for freezing. Therefore a static table with no meaningful dead-tuple pressure still requires wraparound protection.

## 8.10.3 PostgreSQL 18 Freeze Controls

Key controls are:

| Control | PostgreSQL 18 default | Operational role |
|---|---:|---|
| `vacuum_freeze_min_age` | 50 million | Normal XID freezing eligibility |
| `vacuum_freeze_table_age` | 150 million | Influences aggressive freezing scans |
| `autovacuum_freeze_max_age` | 200 million | Forces anti-wraparound maintenance |
| `vacuum_failsafe_age` | 1.6 billion | Activates correctness-first VACUUM failsafe behavior |
| `vacuum_max_eager_freeze_failure_rate` | 0.03 | Controls PostgreSQL 18 eager freezing |

PostgreSQL limits the effective `vacuum_freeze_min_age` relative to `autovacuum_freeze_max_age`, and caps `vacuum_freeze_table_age` at approximately 95% of `autovacuum_freeze_max_age`. These limits preserve maintenance headroom.

Individual relations can also have per-table autovacuum/freeze storage parameters. When a relation enters freezing maintenance earlier than global GUCs appear to predict, inspect `pg_class.reloptions` for intentional policy or configuration drift.

## 8.10.4 Early Versus Late Freezing

Freezing too early can waste work on high-churn tuples that are soon updated and replaced by new tuple versions with new XIDs. Earlier freezing can be useful for immutable data such as completed historical partitions, archived events, audit records, and cold time-series partitions because all-frozen pages reduce future freezing work.

The correct strategy therefore depends partly on relation lifecycle.

## 8.10.5 Normal, Aggressive, and Eager Freezing

Normal VACUUM performs ordinary cleanup and freezes eligible XIDs when it encounters them, but it is not necessarily a full-relation freezing scan. Consequently:

```text
VACUUM completed != every old tuple examined
recent last_autovacuum != guaranteed safe XID age
```

Aggressive VACUUM scans pages that might still contain sufficiently old unfrozen XIDs; pages already known all-frozen can be skipped.

PostgreSQL 18 also supports eager freezing. Normal VACUUM can process selected all-visible but not all-frozen pages, increasing all-frozen coverage and reducing future aggressive-freezing work. Detailed visibility-map semantics are covered in Section 8.9.

## 8.10.6 Insert-Only Relations Still Need Maintenance

Append-oriented relations can require VACUUM even with minimal UPDATE or DELETE activity. PostgreSQL's insert-triggered autovacuum behavior incorporates freezing-related state, so continuous inserts and a growing unfrozen relation can still create maintenance demand. Full autovacuum trigger formulas are covered in Section 8.5.

## 8.10.7 Anti-Wraparound Autovacuum

`autovacuum_freeze_max_age` establishes the principal forced anti-wraparound maintenance boundary. PostgreSQL can initiate anti-wraparound autovacuum even when ordinary autovacuum is disabled:

```text
autovacuum = off != anti-wraparound protection = off
```

An anti-wraparound worker can be recognized in activity reporting by text ending with `(to prevent wraparound)`.

Do not terminate an anti-wraparound worker merely because it consumes significant I/O. First establish current XID age, remaining maintenance and safety runway, VACUUM progress, blockers, storage pressure, and alternative maintenance capacity.

## 8.10.8 VACUUM Failsafe

`vacuum_failsafe_age` adds another correctness-protection layer. PostgreSQL 18 defaults it to approximately 1.6 billion transactions. When XID age becomes dangerously high, VACUUM can enter failsafe behavior that prioritizes completing wraparound-prevention work over some normal maintenance behavior.

The escalation model is:

```text
routine maintenance
    -> anti-wraparound maintenance
    -> VACUUM failsafe
    -> wraparound warnings
    -> critical safety boundary
    -> new XID assignment refused
```

The failsafe threshold is not a normal operating target.

## 8.10.9 Emergency `INDEX_CLEANUP OFF`

When wraparound is genuinely imminent, `VACUUM (INDEX_CLEANUP OFF)` can allow correctness-critical heap/freezing work to finish sooner by deferring index cleanup. This is an emergency optimization, not routine anti-wraparound tuning. Deferred cleanup remains maintenance debt and requires follow-up.

PostgreSQL's automatic VACUUM failsafe should normally be allowed to prioritize completion under dangerous age conditions.

## 8.10.10 `relfrozenxid`

`pg_class.relfrozenxid` represents the relation-level XID freezing horizon. A core metric is:

```sql
age(relfrozenxid)
```

A larger value means the relation is closer to mandatory freezing maintenance.

Do not confuse XID and multixact fields:

```text
XID horizon  -> relfrozenxid
MXID horizon -> relminmxid
```

Detailed multixact behavior is covered in Section 8.11.

## 8.10.11 Horizon Advancement Is the Outcome

A crucial monitoring rule is:

```text
VACUUM completed != relfrozenxid advanced
last_autovacuum is recent != XID safety established
```

Measure the maintenance outcome rather than worker existence or timestamps alone.

## 8.10.12 Include TOAST Relations

Associated TOAST relations maintain their own freezing horizons. Relation-level XID analysis should therefore include both the main relation and its TOAST relation.

Example:

```sql
SELECT
    c.oid::regclass AS table_name,
    greatest(
        age(c.relfrozenxid),
        age(t.relfrozenxid)
    ) AS xid_age
FROM pg_class AS c
LEFT JOIN pg_class AS t
  ON c.reltoastrelid = t.oid
WHERE c.relkind IN ('r', 'm')
ORDER BY xid_age DESC;
```

Ignoring TOAST can hide the relation actually retaining the older horizon.

## 8.10.13 `datfrozenxid`

At database level, `pg_database.datfrozenxid` provides the database freezing horizon. A fleet-level inventory is:

```sql
SELECT
    datname,
    age(datfrozenxid) AS xid_age
FROM pg_database
ORDER BY xid_age DESC;
```

Use database age to identify where to investigate first, then drill down through `relfrozenxid` and associated TOAST state.

## 8.10.14 Planned `VACUUM FREEZE`

PostgreSQL supports:

```sql
VACUUM (FREEZE) public.orders;
```

This requests aggressive freezing behavior and can be useful when deliberately preparing data expected to remain immutable for a long period. It is not the routine answer to every XID-age concern and should not be added automatically to an XID-exhaustion emergency.

## 8.10.15 Maintenance Runway Versus Safety Runway

Absolute XID age is not enough. Two systems with identical XID age can have very different risk when their transaction-consumption rates differ.

Maintenance runway estimates distance or time to a configured forced-maintenance threshold such as `autovacuum_freeze_max_age`. Safety runway represents distance toward PostgreSQL's final wraparound-protection boundary. Reaching the anti-wraparound maintenance threshold does not mean XID assignment shutdown is immediately imminent.

A useful forecast is:

```text
estimated time to threshold
≈ remaining XIDs / recent XID consumption rate
```

Because XID consumption can be bursty, compare short-window, longer-window, and recent peak rates when risk becomes significant.

## 8.10.16 PostgreSQL Safety Boundaries

When a database's oldest unfrozen transaction horizon becomes extremely old, PostgreSQL begins emitting wraparound warnings at roughly 40 million XIDs before wraparound. These warnings are last-line safeguards, not appropriate primary SRE alert thresholds.

If the condition continues until only approximately 3 million XIDs remain, PostgreSQL refuses operations requiring assignment of new transaction IDs. This deliberately sacrifices write availability rather than permit unsafe wraparound.

Existing transactions and read-only work may remain possible, and ordinary VACUUM remains available for recovery.

## 8.10.17 Wraparound Incident Response

Use an evidence-first sequence:

1. Establish severity, configured thresholds, XID consumption rate, maintenance runway, and safety runway.
2. Identify the oldest database using `age(datfrozenxid)`.
3. Identify the oldest main relation and associated TOAST horizon using `age(relfrozenxid)`.
4. Inspect anti-wraparound VACUUM activity and progress.
5. Inspect old prepared transactions.
6. Inspect long-running transactions and old `backend_xid` / `backend_xmin` values.
7. Inspect replication-slot `xmin` and `catalog_xmin` horizons.
8. Remove only validated blockers according to their application or replication semantics.
9. Ensure the administrative role can VACUUM every required relation and system catalog.
10. Run the minimum ordinary VACUUM necessary to advance the freezing horizon.
11. Use emergency optimization such as `INDEX_CLEANUP OFF` only when evidence and remaining runway justify it.
12. Verify `relfrozenxid` advancement.
13. Verify `datfrozenxid` advancement.
14. Confirm that safe runway has been restored.
15. Determine why normal maintenance failed to preserve sufficient runway.

## 8.10.18 Prepared Transactions

Inspect old prepared transactions with:

```sql
SELECT
    gid,
    prepared,
    owner,
    database,
    age(transaction) AS xid_age
FROM pg_prepared_xacts
ORDER BY xid_age DESC;
```

Do not blindly commit or roll back prepared transactions without understanding their application and business state.

## 8.10.19 Long-Running Transactions

Inspect old transaction horizons with `pg_stat_activity`, including `backend_xid`, `backend_xmin`, and transaction start time. Detailed blocker analysis is covered in Section 8.12.

## 8.10.20 Replication Slots

Inspect replication-slot `xmin` and `catalog_xmin` where relevant. An old slot must be traced to its consumer before intervention. Never drop a slot simply because it appears old; doing so can force downstream replicas or logical consumers to be rebuilt or resynchronized. Detailed slot and cleanup-horizon behavior is covered in Section 8.13.

## 8.10.21 Emergency VACUUM Privileges

Emergency database-wide VACUUM must run under a role capable of processing every relation necessary to advance the database horizon, including required system catalogs. Vacuuming user tables while an old catalog remains can leave `datfrozenxid` old.

On managed PostgreSQL services, use the provider-supported administrative role with sufficient privileges rather than assuming traditional superuser semantics.

## 8.10.22 Run the Minimum Required VACUUM

Example:

```sql
VACUUM (VERBOSE) public.orders;
```

During an XID-exhaustion incident the objective is to advance the freezing horizon with the minimum required work.

Do not use `VACUUM FULL` as wraparound remediation. `VACUUM FULL` performs a physical rewrite, introduces stronger locking and additional storage requirements, and solves a different problem. Physical space reclamation is covered in Section 8.16.

Do not automatically add `FREEZE`; unnecessary additional work can be counterproductive during an exhaustion incident.

## 8.10.23 Verify Recovery

After maintenance, recheck database and relation ages. Do not declare resolution because VACUUM completed.

The recovery condition is:

```text
relfrozenxid advances
+
datfrozenxid advances
+
safe runway is restored
```

as applicable to the affected horizon.

## 8.10.24 Determine Why Maintenance Failed

Investigate:

- autovacuum configuration;
- per-table configuration drift;
- worker starvation;
- repeated VACUUM cancellation;
- long transactions;
- prepared transactions;
- replication-slot retention;
- storage latency and insufficient IOPS;
- excessive cost throttling;
- inadequate maintenance windows;
- XID-consumption spikes;
- maintenance-capacity planning.

A wraparound emergency is evidence that normal maintenance failed to preserve sufficient runway.

## 8.10.25 Capacity Planning and Alerting

Production alerting should combine:

```text
absolute XID age
+
percentage of maintenance threshold
+
XID consumption rate
+
estimated maintenance runway
+
estimated safety runway
```

Alert well before PostgreSQL's built-in wraparound warnings.

Increasing `autovacuum_freeze_max_age` is not free postponement. A larger horizon retains more transaction-status history in structures such as `pg_xact` and, when commit timestamp tracking is enabled, `pg_commit_ts`, and can create larger future maintenance events.

## 8.10.26 Production Failure Patterns

### Autovacuum Is Running, Therefore We Are Safe

False. Safety requires the required relations to be processed and their freezing horizons to advance.

### Recent Autovacuum but Old `relfrozenxid`

Recent maintenance timestamps do not prove that the relation's freezing horizon advanced.

### Static Tables Are Forgotten

No UPDATE/DELETE churn does not eliminate freezing requirements for old live tuples.

### Anti-Wraparound Workers Are Repeatedly Killed

This can improve foreground latency temporarily while consuming correctness runway. Treat cancellation as an incident decision.

### `VACUUM FULL` Is Used for XID Recovery

This solves the wrong problem and can make recovery slower and riskier.

### Age Is Monitored Without Consumption Rate

Absolute age alone does not reveal time to risk. Trend XID consumption and forecast runway.

### Global Configuration Looks Safe but One Relation Triggers Early

Inspect `pg_class.reloptions`; per-table freeze policy may explain the behavior.

## 8.10.27 Canonical Production Principles

1. Transaction-ID freezing is a correctness mechanism.
2. Normal PostgreSQL XIDs are finite 32-bit identifiers and wrap around.
3. The two-billion-XID comparison horizon is a conceptual boundary, not an operational target.
4. Old live tuples require freezing even when no dead tuples exist.
5. Dead-tuple cleanup and XID freezing solve different problems.
6. `vacuum_freeze_min_age` controls normal freezing eligibility.
7. `vacuum_freeze_table_age` influences aggressive freezing scans.
8. `autovacuum_freeze_max_age` forces anti-wraparound maintenance.
9. Per-table configuration can make individual relations enter maintenance earlier.
10. Anti-wraparound autovacuum can run even when ordinary autovacuum is disabled.
11. All-frozen pages reduce future aggressive freezing work.
12. PostgreSQL 18 eager freezing distributes some future freezing work across routine VACUUM operations.
13. `vacuum_failsafe_age` adds a correctness-first protection layer.
14. Anti-wraparound workers must not be terminated casually.
15. `relfrozenxid` is the relation-level XID freezing horizon; `relminmxid` is the multixact horizon.
16. TOAST horizons must be included in relation-level XID analysis.
17. Recent VACUUM activity does not prove horizon advancement.
18. Maintenance runway and final safety runway are different concepts.
19. XID consumption rate is essential for forecasting time to risk.
20. PostgreSQL's built-in wraparound warnings are last-line safeguards, not primary SRE alert thresholds.
21. Emergency VACUUM requires sufficient privileges to process every relation holding back the database horizon.
22. Prepared transactions, long-running transactions, and replication slots require evidence-based intervention.
23. `VACUUM FULL` is not an XID-wraparound remediation strategy.
24. `VACUUM FREEZE` should not be added automatically during an XID emergency.
25. `INDEX_CLEANUP OFF` is an emergency optimization, not routine tuning.
26. Successful remediation is proven by horizon advancement and restored runway, not VACUUM completion alone.
27. Freeze-horizon tuning has storage and future-maintenance consequences.
28. Production monitoring should forecast XID runway well before PostgreSQL's final safeguards activate.

## 8.10.29 Key Takeaway

PostgreSQL transaction IDs are finite. As transactions continue, XID age increases and old live tuples must eventually be frozen. PostgreSQL progressively escalates protection from routine maintenance through anti-wraparound maintenance and VACUUM failsafe behavior to final wraparound safeguards that can refuse new XID assignment.

The production model is:

```text
XID age
+
XID consumption rate
+
freeze thresholds
+
VACUUM throughput
+
blocker horizons
+
infrastructure capacity
    -> XID safety runway
```

A healthy system does not merely have autovacuum processes running. Its freezing horizons advance, maintenance completes before correctness deadlines, and sufficient runway remains for safe operation.
