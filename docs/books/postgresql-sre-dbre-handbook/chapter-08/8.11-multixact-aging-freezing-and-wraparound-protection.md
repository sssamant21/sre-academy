# 8.11 — Multixact Aging, Freezing, and Wraparound Protection

**Edition:** Revised Final / Canonical

**Technical Baseline:** PostgreSQL 18

## Purpose

PostgreSQL must protect against both transaction-ID (XID) and Multixact-ID (MXID) wraparound. Multixacts primarily represent multiple transactions participating in compatible tuple locking. A tuple can store an MXID in `xmax`, while PostgreSQL maintains the associated member information separately under `pg_multixact`.

MXID maintenance is a correctness requirement. Production safety depends on advancing relation and database multixact horizons before PostgreSQL reaches forced anti-wraparound or failsafe behavior.

## 8.11.1 XIDs and MXIDs are separate aging systems

XIDs identify transactions and participate in MVCC visibility. MXIDs represent groups of transactions associated primarily with tuple locking. Both identifier spaces are finite, but they have different counters, catalog horizons, storage structures, generation rates, and exhaustion effects.

Do not treat XID age and MXID age as interchangeable metrics.

## 8.11.2 Relation and database horizons

`pg_class.relminmxid` is the relation-level multixact aging boundary. MXIDs older than this horizon have already been replaced in the relation, while MXIDs at or after it may still remain.

`pg_database.datminmxid` provides the database-level horizon.

```sql
SELECT datname, datminmxid, mxid_age(datminmxid) AS mxid_age
FROM pg_database
ORDER BY mxid_age(datminmxid) DESC;
```

```sql
SELECT
    oid::regclass AS relation,
    relminmxid,
    mxid_age(relminmxid) AS mxid_age,
    pg_total_relation_size(oid) AS total_bytes
FROM pg_class
WHERE relkind IN ('r', 'm')
ORDER BY mxid_age(relminmxid) DESC;
```

Successful MXID remediation should demonstrate advancement of the relevant horizon, not merely completion of a VACUUM command.

## 8.11.3 PostgreSQL 18 maintenance defaults

Important PostgreSQL 18 defaults are:

```text
vacuum_multixact_freeze_min_age       = 5,000,000
vacuum_multixact_freeze_table_age     = 150,000,000
autovacuum_multixact_freeze_max_age   = 400,000,000
vacuum_multixact_failsafe_age         = 1,600,000,000
```

These defaults form layered protection and are not universal tuning recommendations.

PostgreSQL constrains the effective minimum and table-age settings relative to `autovacuum_multixact_freeze_max_age`: the minimum-age setting cannot effectively exceed about half of the maximum, while the table-age setting is capped at about 95% of it. The effective failsafe age is maintained at no less than about 105% of the forced-autovacuum maximum.

## 8.11.4 VACUUM and old multixacts

When VACUUM encounters sufficiently old multixact state, PostgreSQL can replace it with the appropriate surviving representation: no remaining lock, a single transaction ID, or a newer multixact representation.

A normal VACUUM can skip pages that do not require inspection. An aggressive VACUUM visits every page that might contain unfrozen XIDs or MXIDs, except pages already known to be all-frozen. This allows PostgreSQL to advance aging horizons reliably.

## 8.11.5 Forced anti-wraparound maintenance

`autovacuum_multixact_freeze_max_age` is the principal forced-maintenance boundary. Protective autovacuum can run even when ordinary autovacuum has been disabled. Correctness protection therefore takes precedence over normal background-maintenance policy.

A worker operating `(to prevent wraparound)` should not be terminated as a routine performance response. Before intervention, establish MXID/XID age, remaining runway, VACUUM progress, blockers, storage headroom, and application impact.

## 8.11.6 MXID risk is two-dimensional

MXID safety is not determined by identifier age alone. PostgreSQL must also manage multixact member storage. One MXID can contain multiple member transactions, so member-space consumption can grow at a different rate from the MXID counter.

PostgreSQL can initiate more frequent aggressive maintenance when multixact member storage grows to roughly 10 GB. The member area can approach roughly 20 GB before its own wraparound boundary becomes critical. These are internal protection boundaries, not production operating targets.

Production monitoring should therefore combine MXID age with `pg_multixact` member-space growth.

## 8.11.7 Lock-heavy workloads

MXID consumption can accelerate under high row-lock concurrency, explicit row locking, queue-table patterns, hot account or inventory rows, application-level serialization, foreign-key-related locking, and retry storms involving tuple locks.

Transaction rate alone is insufficient. Operators must also understand how often workload concurrency creates multi-member tuple-lock states.

## 8.11.8 Consumption velocity and runway

A single MXID-age measurement gives position; operations also need velocity.

Conceptually:

```text
remaining safe MXID budget
--------------------------
observed MXID consumption rate
≈ estimated runway
```

Runway is an estimate because workload behavior can change. Alerting should combine absolute age, percentage of configured safety boundary, consumption velocity, member-space growth, maintenance completion rate, and estimated remaining runway.

## 8.11.9 Failsafe and exhaustion protection

`vacuum_multixact_failsafe_age` is a last-resort protection mechanism. When MXID failsafe activates, PostgreSQL can disable cost-based delay and skip nonessential work such as index vacuuming so that wraparound-prevention work completes faster.

As wraparound approaches, PostgreSQL enters an emergency warning region at roughly 40 million MXIDs remaining. When fewer than roughly 3 million remain, PostgreSQL refuses to create new multixacts.

These are emergency protection boundaries, not acceptable alert thresholds. Production intervention should occur substantially earlier.

## 8.11.10 XID and MXID exhaustion differ

XID exhaustion broadly affects writes requiring new transaction IDs. MXID exhaustion affects operations requiring new multixact state, particularly relevant row-locking operations. Applications can therefore fail selectively depending on locking behavior even while some ordinary writes continue.

## 8.11.11 Replication slots and transactions

Replication slots do not directly retain multixact cleanup horizons in the same manner that they can retain XID-related cleanup horizons. Dropping replication slots is therefore not a generic MXID-remediation action.

Long-running and prepared transactions deserve investigation when they can participate in relevant multixacts, but transaction age alone is not proof of an MXID blocker.

## 8.11.12 Targeted diagnostics

`pg_get_multixact_members()` can inspect the members and lock modes associated with a specific MXID. Use it for targeted diagnosis rather than indiscriminate production-wide scans.

For controlled relation maintenance:

```sql
VACUUM (VERBOSE) public.orders;
```

Then verify:

```sql
SELECT relminmxid, mxid_age(relminmxid)
FROM pg_class
WHERE oid = 'public.orders'::regclass;
```

`VACUUM FULL` is not routine MXID-wraparound remediation. Standard VACUUM is the normal aging mechanism; `VACUUM FULL` adds a table rewrite, `ACCESS EXCLUSIVE` locking, and additional disk requirements.

## 8.11.13 Targeting old relations

`vacuumdb --min-mxid-age` can target relations by multixact age during controlled maintenance. Associated TOAST state must also be considered in risk analysis; `vacuumdb` accounts for associated TOAST state when applying its age filtering.

Avoid excessive parallel maintenance simply because many relations meet an age filter. Relation size, current workload, active protective workers, and infrastructure capacity still matter.

## 8.11.14 Freeze-setting governance

MXID freeze settings directly affect correctness runway. Every override should record the relation, parameter, global and table values, reason, baseline age, consumption rate, member-space condition, expected behavior, owner, implementation date, review date, previous value, and rollback value.

Never weaken a safety setting merely to suppress maintenance impact without understanding the resulting wraparound risk.

## 8.11.15 Production monitoring model

A production dashboard should include database MXID age, oldest relation MXID age, age as a percentage of the forced-maintenance boundary, MXID generation velocity, estimated runway, oldest `relminmxid` relations, `pg_multixact` growth, active and protective autovacuum workers, VACUUM progress, maintenance duration, CPU, storage latency, IOPS, throughput, queue depth, and foreground latency.

The central operational rule is:

> **Do not judge MXID safety by whether autovacuum is running. Judge it by whether the relevant aging horizons are advancing fast enough to preserve or increase remaining runway.**

## 8.11.16 Incident triage

1. Check `pg_database.datminmxid` and `mxid_age(datminmxid)`.
2. Find the relations with the oldest `pg_class.relminmxid`.
3. Consider associated TOAST relations.
4. Check `pg_stat_progress_vacuum`.
5. Identify protective autovacuum workers.
6. Measure `pg_multixact` member-space growth where platform visibility permits.
7. Investigate relevant long-running and prepared transactions and lock-heavy workloads.
8. Check CPU, storage latency, IOPS, throughput, and queue depth.
9. Estimate remaining runway from age and recent consumption velocity.
10. Prefer controlled standard VACUUM and removal of verified blockers.
11. Verify `relminmxid` and, where applicable, `datminmxid` advancement.
12. Correct the underlying workload, maintenance, capacity, or configuration cause.

## 8.11.17 Production principles

- MXID wraparound is a correctness concern.
- XID and MXID aging require separate monitoring.
- `relminmxid` and `datminmxid` are key MXID horizons.
- Aggressive VACUUM periodically advances aging horizons.
- Protective maintenance can run even when ordinary autovacuum is disabled.
- MXID age and member-space consumption are separate safety dimensions.
- Lock-heavy workloads can accelerate both.
- Do not terminate anti-wraparound VACUUM casually.
- MXID failsafe can override normal performance-oriented throttling.
- Replication slots are not generic MXID blockers.
- Old transactions are not automatically MXID blockers.
- `VACUUM FULL` is not routine MXID remediation.
- Successful remediation requires verified horizon advancement.
- Freeze overrides require strict governance.
- Alert on runway well before emergency boundaries.


## Key Takeaway

Multixact safety depends on both identifier aging and member-space consumption. Healthy operation requires VACUUM to advance MXID horizons fast enough to preserve substantial runway before PostgreSQL enters anti-wraparound, failsafe, warning, or exhaustion-protection states.
